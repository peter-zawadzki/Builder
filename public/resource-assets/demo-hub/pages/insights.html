3 things to work on
1
Build Hip-Shoulder Separation in Right Turns
In right turns your hips are rotating with your shoulders instead of staying countered downhill. Try to keep your chest and outside hand pointing more toward the fall line as you tip into each right turn. Think of your upper body as a stable platform and let only your lower body carve through the arc.
2
Commit Earlier to the Edge — Especially in Right Turns
Several right turns show the apex occurring late (past 55% of the turn), meaning you are not fully on edge until you are already past the gate. Try to feel the ski bite earlier — imagine pressing the outside ski into the snow from the very start of each arc rather than waiting until you are alongside the gate.
3
Stabilize Shoulder Tilt Through All Turns
Your shoulders are tilting quite actively (mean absolute tilt around 12°, with a range up to 56°) and shoulders are level only about 20% of the time. Focus on keeping your shoulders more parallel to the slope — a quiet upper body will help you hold your edge angle longer and give you more consistent arc pressure from start to finish.
What's working
You are producing solid peak edge angles — regularly reaching 58–65° on right turns — which shows real commitment to carving. Your overall apex timing averages right in the ideal 40–50% window, meaning you are generally hitting the gate at the right moment in the arc. Hip-shoulder separation on left turns is genuinely strong, consistently above the 2.0 target, which is a clear technical strength to build from.
